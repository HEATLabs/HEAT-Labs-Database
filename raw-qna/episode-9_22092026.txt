Q: **What is the story behind ABV?**
A: (Rickles) So before we talk about ABV, let’s start with where you put the ABV, which is on Ember as a character. We had intended on having 2 vehicles for Ember on launch, both the XM1V and the ABV. ABV had some unique features, which - to solve the performance of - took us a little bit longer than we would’ve liked, so it pushed back. Ember as a character - themed around the aspect of area control and essentially if you were to image where you’d want to put this character in an environment, if there is - a bottleneck that the enemy has to tank or an objective zone they need to clear; Ember wants to position herself as the gatekeeper to it. She is not great at pushing objective, but once she is in there - kinda hard to dig her out. Survivability is not as great as the other defenders - wants to avoid getting shot at by things at long range. Marksman will absolutely do horrible things to both of her vehicles, but when she gets in a brawl - she will stick you in a locker and probably pin you in there until the rest of the team are dog-piles you, and it’s going to be a very messy story.

Now, the ABV - how did that come into existence? So, middle of last year, creative directors picking vehicles - we like to give our players non-conventional combat vehicles in WoT: HEAT. When we have a vehicle with a gun on it, and looks like a tank, and it behaves like a tank - trying to introduce a variety in the game becomes more difficult. So for the combat engineer perspective, the ABV - it's an Armor Breaching Vehicle. This is the vehicle they put in an armored battalion, and it’s meant for crossing fortifications. It's probably the vehicle you the least want to be assigned as a tanker. They only got about 4 of these in a battalion, so the responsibility is pretty high, and you basically drive ahead of everybody else and everything is going to be shooting at you.

The vehicle visually - we wanted to match the real world representation on it, so there is this turret with a very-very large box on the back, and that box contains about 3 tonnes of C4 packed in coiled lines, and it’s meant for clearing minefields. How do you represent this as something you use in-game? And that was a difficult challenge. Problem being that - we didn’t really have from an animation standpoint - a setup where we could have this vehicle be able to move and shoot at the same time. Doing the soft body animations for the line charges is quite difficult, which is why it has to stop right now. People are going to ask right now the question of “Why does it have to be stationary?” - so that doesn’t look janky. It was the compromise we had to make there, and then in real life actually, the vehicle does have to get stationary to use its weapon.

Now what is a line charge doing to HEAT’s vehicles? Well, we went for removing external components, so shedding your ERA and tracks, and a bit of damage. If the breacher can hold you in one spot - great. Means that from a team play perspective - making somebody immobile is usually a death sentence. Assuming that Ember has got support, given she is played defensively.

And as people pointed out - there were a lot of effects, visual noise was one of the main contentious points of feedback. Was that intended? From the flamethrower - no. Problem for the breacher is - she doesn’t go very fast, and she has got a very short stick to beat you with. She can’t reach you, it’s kind of a one-way interaction.

Now, Marksman are supposed to be a hard counter to this vehicle. Ember does not want to be in the open with it. How do you give the vehicle a counter-play tool? This is where those Incendiary Launchers come in. People use it for applying a status effect. Now, that wasn’t really the intent of the vehicle to start with. It was meant to be an augmented (better) version of our smoke launchers that are currently exist in a game. Blocks thermal vision, and with that - Ember can buy a little bit of time whilst out in the open, to try and get itself safe before it gets nuked by someone like Leopard 2FK or 1A6.

The playtesting - as to how people place these, then they get used at far closer range than we would like. Once you’re inside this combat envelope of close range engagements of about 50 to 60 meters. This, as an effect; really shouldn’t be something that’s used. And this is something we’ll come back to in a little while (see Q№). But we have looked at the feedback on how these visual effects are being intrusive to combat environment that we would not like them to be, which is this role scenario. They are still going to be a meaningful tool we want the Breacher to have against Marksmen. It is a very much a “feast or famine” (basically no middle-ground solution) sort of vehicle, so they will be staying around, but there will be changes

Q: **Did you guys think of an idea of giving her (ABV) an actual gun and making a flamethrower an ability for example? **
A: (Rickles) Having an ability as a flamethrower? Yeah, it could have been done. Why didn’t we [do it]? Well they wanted the flamethrower to be one of the forth-front features of the tank. It's why it's taken the place of the main gun. In reality this vehicle, as I said, has a .50 calibre machine gun on top. To have given it a gun and a flamethrower - it’s  a lot of versatility. Problem is - if we were going to give it a gun, it would have probably been something along the lines of AP.

Problem is - if we gave it a gun that has the option to immobilise the vehicles, being held in place is bad. Being held in place over a pit of fire is a lot worse. The actual tools kit that the Breacher has in order to do this - is a little bit more limited. You have this Line Charge Launcher, it can immobilise you, but it’s a skill shot. There is a lot of televised VFX for the Line Charge Launch, [so it] gives you some time to react. You can get out before this thing detonates, and you won't be immobilised. If it does catch you, and you’re in a pit of fire - you get to sit there for a couple of seconds and burn, and it’s not a very pleasant experience. So this is one of the reasons why we didn’t actually give it a gun + a flamethrower.

Q: **How is it different from balancing such a tank from a shell-focused tanks? **
A: (Rickles) So, having done the ballistics systems for the other shells, I can tell you that all of our shells in-game are basically balanced around this concept of a single shot loaded with AP. Now if you look closely at the values for your load time and your shell damage, you divide the damage by the load time, so Abrams will get you 120. Okay, so the raw damage of all of our AP loaders is 120 DPS. Obviously some guns fire quicker than others. There are some additional modifiers that can come from a loader, whether it's an autoloader or a burst loader. So, we already have this kind of target number that we were aiming for, for the Breacher. Although the flamethrower is quite Unique in the fact that it almost acts as true damage. It ignores armour entirely.

So, whilst you might not have an internal damage system with the flamethrower, so all of a sudden, you’re missing out on a lot of potential damage; but your consistency is very good. Now, the fire effect itself - when set on fire, you are taking damage ticks of 20 every .25 second, so it’s an 80 DPS comes just from the status effects, once it’s triggered on you, so it’s our starting point as to how we go about balancing the actual damage output of the weapon. We take the 80 DPS from the status effect into account and then the amount of damage that comes from hit on the flamethrower itself as well. This flamethrower has a fire rate of .15 seconds. The hit damage is quite low, but when you combine it with the status effect damage, you get a little bit more than 120 DPS.

As I said, you don’t get any critical damage [multipliers] and that basically brings you up to the values that you’re currently seeing in live except - when vulnerability status effect gets applied. And there’s a big with the vulnerability status effect - it multiplies it (damage) twice. So it should only be giving you ~80% extra damage. It’s currently doing about ~160% extra. That was a bug that made it through. [It] has resulted in people seeing something like 400 DPS being thrown at them point-blank. And when you are on the receiving end, [and] it ignores your armor, unless you have invulnerability to fire damage, which is another thing; you melt pretty fast. So that was an undesirable outcome and of course once players found it - you’re more than happy to use it against each other.
